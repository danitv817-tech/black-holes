01 — Definition

GRAVITY
GONE
INFINITE

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so extreme that nothing — not matter, not energy, not light itself — can escape once it crosses the event horizon.

They form when massive stars collapse at the end of their lives, compressing into an infinitely dense singularity that warps space and time itself.

02 — Structure

ANATOMY
OF THE VOID

SINGULARITY
The central point of infinite density and zero volume. All the mass is compressed here. Physics as we know it completely breaks down.
EVENT HORIZON
The point of no return. Once anything crosses this boundary, escape is impossible — not even light can climb back out.
PHOTON SPHERE
A zone where gravity is strong enough to force photons (light particles) to travel in circular orbits around the black hole.
ACCRETION DISK
A superheated ring of gas and dust spiraling inward. It glows with intense radiation — this is what we see in real black hole images.
RELATIVISTIC JETS
Twin beams of plasma ejected from the poles at near light speed, stretching for thousands of light-years into space.
03 — Classification

THREE KINDS
OF DARKNESS

TYPE / 01
STELLAR
3 — 20× Solar Mass
The most common type, formed when a massive star exhausts its nuclear fuel and its core collapses in a violent supernova explosion.
TYPE / 02
INTERMEDIATE
100 — 100,000× Solar Mass
Rare and elusive, found in dense star clusters. Believed to be the missing link between stellar and supermassive black holes.
TYPE / 03
SUPERMASSIVE
Millions — Billions × Solar Mass
Lurking at the center of almost every large galaxy. Our own Milky Way hosts Sagittarius A* — 4 million solar masses.
04 — History

TIMELINE OF
DISCOVERY

1783
John Michell — Dark Stars
First theoretical prediction: a star massive enough would trap even light with its gravity.
1915
Einstein's General Relativity
Einstein publishes equations describing how mass curves spacetime — the mathematical foundation for black holes.
1967
John Wheeler Coins the Term
"Black hole" replaces "gravitationally completely collapsed object" in a Wheeler lecture.
1974
Hawking Radiation Theory
Stephen Hawking theorizes that black holes slowly emit thermal radiation and will eventually evaporate.
2015
First Gravitational Waves
LIGO detects ripples from two merging black holes 1.3 billion light-years away — a new way to observe the universe.
2019
First Image of a Black Hole
The Event Horizon Telescope captures M87* — 6.5 billion solar masses, 55 million light-years away.
2022
Sagittarius A* Photographed
EHT photographs the black hole at the center of our own Milky Way galaxy for the first time.
05 — Strange Truths

MIND-BENDING
FACTS

01
If you fell in, you would experience spaghettification — tidal forces would stretch your body into a strand of subatomic particles.
02
To an outside observer, you would freeze at the event horizon forever — gravitational time dilation means you never quite fall in.
03
Black holes are not cosmic vacuum cleaners. If the Sun became one, Earth's orbit would not change — only the light would vanish.
04
A black hole with the Sun's mass would be only 6 km in diameter — smaller than a city, yet containing a star's worth of mass.
05
TON 618, the largest known black hole, has a mass of 66 billion suns — its event horizon is larger than our entire solar system.
06
Hawking radiation predicts black holes slowly evaporate. A stellar black hole would take 10⁶⁷ years to completely disappear.
